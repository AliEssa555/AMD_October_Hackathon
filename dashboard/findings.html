The router that never routed

We reproduced YOLO-DER: A Dynamic Enhancement Routing Framework (Electronics 2025) and instrumented its core mechanism instead of only scoring it. The router assigns near-identical weights to every path for every image. When forced to choose, detection collapses.

Finding 01

Push the router harder and it does commit — then the detector dies

The paper calls DER “entropy-aware” but never optimises any entropy. Its own remedy, an entropy penalty, was configured at 0.01 — which we measured as roughly 0.3% of the detection loss, far too weak to move anything. Sweeping it properly separates two things that had been assumed to travel together: whether the router can commit, and whether committing helps.

The control that makes this attributable. The penalty acts on every routing distribution, so EnhanceNet’s gate collapsed alongside the router and the damage could have belonged to either. Targeting DER alone separates them: at weight 5.0 the router collapses to entropy 0.0063 while the gate stays healthy at 0.6774, and mAP50 still falls to 0.3163. The collapse is the router’s.
Finding 02

Routing is uniform in every variant; the enhancement gate never is

Finding 03

Nothing beats the baseline, and only the collapses are measurable

Three seeds of the baseline span 0.0115 mAP50. That spread is the noise floor, and every architectural delta in the study is smaller than it — including an extension of our own that looked like a win on a single seed.

Method

How the measurement works

Normalised routing entropy

H(p) / log N, where p is the softmax over N paths for one image. 0 means the router commits to one path; 1 means it averages them all. Dividing by log N makes a 4-path router comparable with a 5-branch gate.

Why the penalty was too weak

The logged term was 0.01498, which is exactly 0.01 × mean(3·log4, 3·log5) — the mean entropy of three 4-path routers and three 5-branch gates at initialisation. Against a detection loss of order 2–6 it contributes about 0.3% of the gradient.

Hardware provenance

Every run carries gpu=AMD Instinct MI300X VF and hip=6.4.43483 as MLflow tags. Measured throughput: 765 img/s on ExDark, 1,056 img/s on BDD100K at 448 px, 3.8 GB of 192 GB VRAM.

Deviations from the paper. YOLOv8n rather than YOLOv12, 448 px, batch 64, no L_enh term (ExDark has no clean/degraded pairs, so it is not computable). Single seed for most variants; three for the baseline and for the severity-conditioned extension.
